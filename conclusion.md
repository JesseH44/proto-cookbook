# Results

In this cookbook, we covered baseline data interaction through the [**PIKART**](https://ar.pik-potsdam.de/?a=general) database. This database is curated from ERA5 reanalysis data, but has been filtered to include only data pertinent to atmospheric rivers, with special calculated variables pertaining to atmospheric river strength such as intensity, duration, and rating. The main use case for this database is to analyze historical atmospheric rivers from 1940-2023. Atmospheric river events can transport huge amounts of water over vast distances, with high potential for flooding events especially in high-value areas such as the west coast of the United States. Due to the risk presented by atmospheric rivers, careful examination of their formation and impacts must be studied.

In this cookbook, there are two notebooks; accessing the data and loading it in convenient formats, as well as using the data for analysis and visualization. The data-access.ipynb notebook is meant to be an introductory notebook into loading the data from PIKART. This is expanded on in the next notebook which involves an example workflow for examining a specific atmospheric river event that occurred from December 26, 1996 to January 3, 1997. This atmospheric river was rated AR5, the highest category for an atmospheric river, based on the amount of water it holds and it's rate of transport. This atmospheric river caused devastating flooding along the west coast. In the visualization.ipynb notebook, we are able to determine the origin of this atmospheric river through a dynamic visualization. The atmospheric river originated from the central pacific, near Hawaii, thus classifying this atmospheric river as an example of a common phenomena in atmospheric rivers known as the 'pineapple express'. This is named due to the high frequency of atmospheric rivers that originate near Hawaii and transport water to the west coast. The dynamic visualization displayed the origin of the atmospheric river and it's evolution over time, using a time slider. We can see the atmospheric river expand it's flow from the main island of Hawaii towards Northern California and Oregon.
